Data

Sources

  • IRS Form 990 e-file XML releases: the returns themselves, including officers, directors, trustees, key employees and their compensation.
  • IRS Exempt Organizations Business Master File: organization names, EINs, locations and status.
  • ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer API, where used, to cross-check organizations and link to filings.
  • IPUMS NHGIS: census and American Community Survey tables by state, county, place and tract. Jonathan Schroeder, David Van Riper, Steven Manson, Grace Cooper, Zachary Krause, Tracy Kugler, Tsu Zhu, and Steven Ruggles. IPUMS National Historical Geographic Information System: Version 21.0 [dataset]. Minneapolis, MN: IPUMS. 2026. http://doi.org/10.18128/D050.V21.0. Explore it under Places and the census: every state, county, city and tract with the nonprofits, people and other records in it, and census leaderboards.
  • FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis: the local economy by county and state (the latest monthly unemployment rate, per capita and total personal income, GDP, population, house price index, median household income and poverty estimates), metro-area unemployment, and the national series (CPI, unemployment, GDP, wages, home prices, interest rates), loaded through the FRED API and kept current weekly. Shown as “Economy now” on every census area page and in “The area” on organization and other entity pages, each figure linked to its FRED series; the CPI deflates dollars across years (“in today’s dollars”). Source: FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis; FRED republishes the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the Bureau of Economic Analysis, the Census Bureau and the Federal Housing Finance Agency. This product uses the FRED® API but is not endorsed or certified by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (terms of use). API: /api/v1/fred/{series_id} and the fred dataset under Developers.

Want the data in bulk? Use the IRS source files linked above, or email info@furp.org.

Beyond nonprofit returns, the Browse menu lists every other public-records dataset here, and Developers describes each one for the API. The newest: IPO registrations (SEC Forms S-1 and F-1), every registration statement in EDGAR since 1993 with its amendments, final prospectus and effectiveness notice, tied to the company’s insider filings, Form D offerings and other records on this site. Data: SEC EDGAR.

Files that named people upload to the FURP space and mark Public are read into the database after security checks and listed under Documents, with their text, tables and the people and organizations they mention matched to the records here; they are the documents dataset of the API. Data: uploaded by the named person, who is responsible for it.

Coverage

  • E-filed returns only. Organizations that filed on paper are absent.
  • Small organizations that file the 990-N postcard report only a principal officer, shown on their page with their exempt type, ruling date and status; they have no other people data here.
  • Coverage starts with the IRS's first public e-file XML release (2011), loaded newest first. Older years are still being added.

Refresh schedule

Every night we check the IRS for newly released filings, load them, and re-link people across filings. The IRS releases e-filed returns in batches, usually monthly, so a return typically appears here a few months after it was filed.

Name matching

Many people share a name. People with the same name are shown as separate records unless the evidence links them, such as the same organization, an overlapping title, or a consistent address across filings.

Every link between records shows its evidence, so you can judge it yourself. When the evidence is thin, we keep the records apart rather than guess.

Grants and donor-advised funds

  • Grants to organizations come from Schedule I Part II of the Form 990 (recipient, the EIN the grantor reported, city and state, cash and non-cash amounts, purpose) and from the grants-paid list on the 990-PF (which has no EIN column; we match those to organizations only by exact name and state). Grants to individuals are counted, but their names are not kept. Some large foundations attach their grant list as a separate document instead of filling in the e-file table; those grants are not in the data.
  • Donor-advised funds: the Form 990 Part IV question (did the organization maintain donor advised funds) and Schedule D Part I: the number of funds, contributions, grants, and year-end value, for donor-advised funds and for other funds and accounts (the second column only from 2013-era returns on).
  • Donors to individual donor-advised funds are not public. These filings show what the sponsor reports, not who gave. We do not try to work out who funds a donor-advised fund.
  • Where the money went: every expense line on each return: Form 990 Part IX with its program, management and fundraising columns and the organization's own descriptions of its “other” expenses; 990-EZ Part I; 990-PF Part I. Lines the organization describes as events (galas, dinners, golf, auctions, conferences and the like) are marked, and organization pages add them to conferences and meetings and the direct costs of fundraising events. 990-EZ “other expenses” are itemized only on Schedule O, which we don't read.
  • Also read from each return: Schedule L loans to and from officers and other interested persons, and the 990-PF payout figures (Part X minimum investment return, Part XII qualifying distributions).

Cross-references

{%- set xs = xl.summary() if xl is defined else none %}

Every record here is cross-referenced to every other: a continuous engine (loader/xref.py) links nonprofits, people, contractors, federal award recipients, lobbying clients and registrants, political committees, SEC filers and insiders, Form D issuers, advisers, Form 5500 sponsors, Open Payments companies, FARA registrants, state business registrations, recall firms, patent and trademark owners, colleges, IPO registrants and underwriters and student-loan issuers, by identifier (EIN, CIK, CRD), by name and state, by name and city, by street address, and people by full name to the one person page with that name in that state; and every record to its interests (NTEE code, FURP topic, lobbying issue, industry, patent class, federal program), so records with the same interests find each other across datasets. A sweep over new records runs every two hours; a full rebuild runs weekly off peak. Every link names its rule on the page it appears on, with EXACT or FUZZY; a shared name or address is never proof of the same person or organization.
