Data

Sources

  • IRS Form 990 e-file XML releases: the returns themselves, including officers, directors, trustees, key employees and their compensation.
  • IRS Exempt Organizations Business Master File: organization names, EINs, locations and status.
  • ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer API, where used, to cross-check organizations and link to filings.

Want the data in bulk? Use the IRS source files linked above, or email info@strategystudio.org.

Coverage

  • E-filed returns only. Organizations that filed on paper are absent.
  • Small organizations that file the 990-N postcard report no people, so they have no people data here.
  • Coverage starts with the IRS's first public e-file XML release (2011), loaded newest first. Older years are still being added.

Refresh schedule

Every night we check the IRS for newly released filings, load them, and re-link people across filings. The IRS releases e-filed returns in batches, usually monthly, so a return typically appears here a few months after it was filed.

Name matching

Many people share a name. People with the same name are shown as separate records unless the evidence links them, such as the same organization, an overlapping title, or a consistent address across filings.

Every link between records shows its evidence, so you can judge it yourself. When the evidence is thin, we keep the records apart rather than guess.

Grants and donor-advised funds

  • Grants to organizations come from Schedule I Part II of the Form 990 (recipient, the EIN the grantor reported, city and state, cash and non-cash amounts, purpose) and from the grants-paid list on the 990-PF (which has no EIN column; we match those to organizations only by exact name and state). Grants to individuals are counted, but their names are not kept. Some large foundations attach their grant list as a separate document instead of filling in the e-file table; those grants are not in the data.
  • Donor-advised funds: the Form 990 Part IV question (did the organization maintain donor advised funds) and Schedule D Part I: the number of funds, contributions, grants, and year-end value, for donor-advised funds and for other funds and accounts (the second column only from 2013-era returns on).
  • Donors to individual donor-advised funds are not public. These filings show what the sponsor reports, not who gave. We do not try to work out who funds a donor-advised fund.
  • Where the money went: every expense line on each return: Form 990 Part IX with its program, management and fundraising columns and the organization's own descriptions of its “other” expenses; 990-EZ Part I; 990-PF Part I. Lines the organization describes as events (galas, dinners, golf, auctions, conferences and the like) are marked, and organization pages add them to conferences and meetings and the direct costs of fundraising events. 990-EZ “other expenses” are itemized only on Schedule O, which we don't read.
  • Also read from each return: Schedule L loans to and from officers and other interested persons, and the 990-PF payout figures (Part X minimum investment return, Part XII qualifying distributions).
