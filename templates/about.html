About

Who

Built and run by the FURP Alliance. It is a labor of love: a tool for checking what nonprofits, and now campaigns, contractors and corporate insiders, report in public records, opened up for anyone to use.

Why

People say they run nonprofits, sit on boards, and draw no salary. Checking takes an hour of digging through IRS filings, so most people don't bother.

Sometimes the people doing it deserve our thanks. Other times they want to move the in-ground pool a few feet and fly first class, or at least business.

Because of abuse, people stop giving. Sunlight is the best disinfectant, and we hope this makes it easier to give to the charities that deserve it.

Type a name and see every Form 990 that person appears on, with the filing linked so you can read it yourself. It's free, it's open source, and it's much faster than asking Claude, ChatGPT or the others.

What you can do

  • Search people by name and see every organization, title, year and dollar of pay.
  • Ask questions in plain text, such as "Who runs the Red Cross?"
  • Find organizations by name, or list every organization in a city or state.
  • Open organization pages with finances, people, contractors, grants made and received, and where the money went.
  • Find donor-advised fund sponsors and the grants foundations paid.
  • See who funds an organization, organizations similar to it, and the funders that back similar organizations. Funders come from the grants other filers list: foundations on the 990-PF and charities, including donor-advised fund sponsors, on Schedule I. Similar means the same or a closely related NTEE code and a comparable budget.
  • Browse the leaderboards, from the highest-paid people to fundraising event costs.
  • Copy any large table, or download a leaderboard as CSV.
  • Open each filing, with a link to the original 990.
  • Coverage: tax years 2010 to 2025, e-filed returns only.

What it is not

  • Not affiliated with the IRS or ProPublica.
  • Not legal, financial or tax advice.
  • Not more accurate than the filings. The data is only as good as what organizations reported to the IRS.

The underlying filings remain public records at their sources (the IRS). Data compiled by third parties keeps its own terms.
